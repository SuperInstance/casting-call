## What FLARE is

FLARE ("Diffusion for Hybrid Language Model", arXiv:2606.01774, Zhu et al. — Adobe Research and Georgia Tech; v1 1 June 2026, v2 4 Aug 2026) is a **conversion framework**, not a model family release. It converts strong autoregressive checkpoints with hybrid attention (the Qwen3.5 line) into dual-mode models that support both AR-style verified decoding ("AR-Trust") and diffusion-style parallel denoising ("Diffusion-Trust") from a single checkpoint, served under one SGLang-based stack. The paper trains FLARE-2B/4B/9B from the post-trained Qwen3.5-2B/4B/9B checkpoints in a single SFT stage on roughly 10B tokens, with a token-balanced joint AR-and-diffusion objective (block size 4). Its headline finding is methodological: **transfer-data quality is the primary determinant of whether a converted checkpoint keeps its seed capability** — outweighing loss formulation and attention-mask design.

## Why no bench numbers

Every other entry in this catalogue grades a served model on the fixed battery, a Mastermind fixture, and a writing sample. None of that is possible here: the only FLARE artefacts in the world are Adobe/Georgia Tech's internal Qwen3.5 conversions. Reproducing FLARE from the paper alone would mean re-running a 10B-token conversion training run — a research project, not a bench session, and out of scope for a casting call.

## Casting note

FLARE cannot be cast because it cannot be run — full stop. Its value to this bench is the transferable finding rather than a candidate: if the fleet ever converts one of its resident AR checkpoints (the Qwen3.5-9B already on this box is exactly FLARE's seed class) into a dual-mode diffusion model, FLARE's result says to spend the effort on the quality of the transfer data before touching the loss or the masks. Worth re-auditing if Adobe releases the FLARE checkpoints; the entry will be replaced by a served bench run the week that happens.
