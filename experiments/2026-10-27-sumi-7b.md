## Addendum — process record and reading notes (same day)

Technique: Sumi is a **uniform** diffusion language model, pretrained from
scratch (1.5T tokens) on the GIDD framework with an SNR reparameterisation.
The distinction that matters: in masked diffusion a token, once filled, can
never be revised; in uniform diffusion **any token can be updated at any
step** — the canvas stays negotiable until the schedule ends. That is the
property this bench's revisability spectrum cares about, and it is why the
model rewards a second look despite the score below: architecturally it is
the most re-readable sampler here. (A sibling fleet lane studying
revision-vs-correction found the same model's editing behaviour to be
churn — writes far exceeding net change, A→B→A — which is the shadow side
of the same property: a canvas that can always change does not always
settle.)

The measured character on this box:

- **One price per request.** Every battery probe — 200-token and 650-token
  budgets alike — took 226.0–226.4 s. The sampler settles a fixed
  2048-token canvas over a fixed 128 denoising steps, so a request costs
  one full settle regardless of what is asked. Tokens are nearly free;
  requests are not. Any system built on this model must batch its
  questions accordingly.
- **The canvas is a hard wall, and two bench fixtures hit it.** The
  12k-token bench prompt is not slow but impossible
  (`prompt_length (11395) leaves no room in canvas_length (2048)`;
  bench.py exit 1). The writings step failed the same way by 118 tokens
  (prompt 2166 > 2048) and returned in 4 s with nothing to publish. A
  2048-token working surface is the model's shipped shape; nothing was
  reconfigured to evade it.
- Battery 2/7, both passes in the continuation register a base model is
  good at: naming Canberra inside a question-shaped prompt, and 417 words
  of open-ended prose inside the word band with the banned word absent.
